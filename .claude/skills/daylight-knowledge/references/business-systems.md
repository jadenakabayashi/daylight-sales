## CPM / rate decisioning — data points, views, and sources

Synthesized 2026-07-27 by pulling together Thomas's own described workflow (`journeys/sales/notes.md`) with the newer Vault build-session detail (`vault-product-development.md`) — nothing new here, just consolidated so CPM questions don't require re-deriving this from three separate files each time.

**Inputs that feed a CPM/rate decision:**
- **Sellout / Fill Status %** — the core driver. Thomas looks at it both trailing (~8 weeks back) and forward (~90 days), because "the past has to tell you the future." Rising sellout over time = raise the rate (explicit example: a show accepted at 55% sellout when it first onboarded now expects/gets 85%).
- **Order velocity/trend** — how many orders came in over the last ~2 weeks; a burst (e.g. 4 orders one week, 0 the next) signals a show "getting hot" and is treated as a signal to move rate, independent of raw sellout %.
- **AUR (Average Unit Rate)** — the show's actual average sold rate, which Thomas explicitly distinguishes from the Rate Card's *listed* rate — real example given: Rate Card listed $10,000 for a show whose real average sold price was $6,900. Decisions should follow the real sold average, not a stale list price. AUR is planned to become a formal, forward-looking (90-day) figure on a dedicated Analytics tab (`vault-product-development.md`), not just memory.
- **Pricing floor vs. negotiable range** — a hard floor per show (juniors like "Jay Rose" are only shown/allowed the floor), with an above-floor negotiating range reserved for senior judgment (Thomas). The Rate Card figure sits at the top of that range, not as a fixed price.
- **Delivery/attribution data (Podscribe)** — used to check over/under-delivery, which feeds back into whether a rate holds, needs a make-good, or justifies an increase.
- **Competitor/category context** — see the exclusivity-tier breakdown in `vault-product-development.md`; knowing who else is already on a show (and at what volume) affects what's negotiable.
- **Advertiser history (18-24 months)** — for negotiating *with* a specific client rather than pricing a show in the abstract: past shows bought, currently-running annuals, re-up/testing-phase candidates, total dollars spent, count of repeat placements, and the advertiser's own competitor activity. Thomas explicitly said this — not the show-level rate page — is what he actually wants pulled up on a live sales call.
- **Which agency placed a given spot** — a currently-missing data point Thomas flagged directly: the existing "direct AR" delivery view doesn't show which agency is buying a given advertiser's spots, which he wants surfaced (useful for tracking agency-level spend/relationships, and agencies now can have simultaneous relationships with more than one advertiser per the new agency-advertiser relationship model).

**Views/pages, in the order Thomas says he actually uses them ("the Bible" chain, per the raw transcript):**
1. **Advertiser dashboard / "direct AR" page** — pulled up per-advertiser, 18-24 months of history; Thomas calls this page "the Bible" and starts every negotiation-prep here.
2. A second page he described only as sounding like **"the law"** in the auto-transcript — given the same conversation's separate discussion of a pricing floor, this is plausibly a mis-transcription of **"the floor"** (a floor/range reference page); unconfirmed, worth checking against the recording rather than trusting the transcript literally.
3. **Fill Rate** page/view (mis-transcribed as "Phil Ray" in the source — matches the general "Fill status" → "Phil status" transcription pattern seen elsewhere).
4. **Delivery report, per spot** — the per-spot Podscribe-backed delivery numbers.
- Thomas's own framing: he needs "five or six data points" across these pages before he can make a pricing decision, and today keeps 4-5 browser tabs open simultaneously (Molly/Marly + Bob) to do it — the explicit motivation behind Vault's unified Ad Plan/delivery-report merge (see `vault-product-development.md`).
- The **Analytics tab** (planned, not yet built) is meant to become the consolidated home for Fill Status, AUR, and RPM together, replacing this multi-tab hop.

**Sources these data points ultimately come from:**
- **The Rate Card / Roster Excel file** (`refs/Foundation/Daylight Media Roster 7.13.26.xlsx`) — Net CPM, Net Rate, Position, Current Advertisers, and the fuller confirmed column set in the Rate Card entry above.
- **Podscribe** — delivery/attribution numbers, both for Thomas's own CPM-relevance checks and for the advertiser-history view.
- **Thomas's own memory/judgment** — explicitly still the primary mechanism today for weighing all of the above into an actual number; nothing here is a formula yet, current system support is close to none.
- **RPM** (`vault-product-development.md`) — a newer, not-yet-built show-health metric (revenue against aggregated cross-channel consumption) intended to sit alongside Fill Status/AUR on the show page, but not itself a CPM input yet — treat as a related-but-separate metric, not part of today's actual rate-setting process.



- **Papering** — team jargon for turning a confirmed/signed deal into a live order record in Marly/QCode. Handoff channel is email (no automated intake). Order naming convention: `{order#}_{advertiser}_{agency or client}_{date}`, e.g. `2433_Tecovas_Oxford Road_5/26/2026`. Order status includes at least `Booked` (green) and `Needs Counter Sign` (orange) — the latter is the "hold" state: papered and entered, but not counted as real booked revenue until countersignature comes back. There are likely more status values than these two observed so far — don't assume the list is complete.
- **RFP → vetting → submission flow** — confirmed via two independent sources now (`Sales & Ad Ops Flow.pdf` and `refs/Foundation/Sales & Ad Ops Flow.pptx`, same content, different export): RFP intake → Competitive Advertiser Check (exclusivity: 3 embedded spots or 3 months of DAI impressions secures it; holds 30 days after last ad airs) → Check Previous Vetting Response → filter Rate Card to RFP criteria → vet remaining shows, log "whys" back onto the Rate Card → submit (client's own template if they have one, else email) → client feedback (none/some/all shows).
- **The RFP/rate-card submission template is far richer than the flow doc implies.** `refs/Foundation/OR 2026 RFP Template (3).xlsx` (blank, Oxford-Road-specific) has separate Embedded-media (flat rate) and Dynamic-media (CPM/DAI) column groups, audio/video/Spotify-video split, full audience demo block (age/gender/HHI/geo), cancellation clause, exclusivity type + minimum requirements, pixel/ad-server compatibility, and a discounts/rationale column — this is the realistic shape of "transfer rate-card data into the client's template," not just a few columns.
