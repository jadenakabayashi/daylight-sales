## Process reconstruction (current state)

1. **Show onboarding into Marly** — **now firsthand, confirmed by Ryan Middledorf himself** (previously secondhand via Jay). Ryan owns onboarding for every new show: ~20-30 data points collected via a checklist plus live onboarding calls walking talent/producers through best practices and ad-sales procedures, then a talent survey and a separate show survey to populate Marly. Both surveys carry outdated fields (Ryan: some "don't necessarily need to be here anymore") and are missing newer categories like supplements and AI-ad approvals. Ryan's own fix-it list: a single "invitation to vault" link so producers/talent can complete every onboarding step with one sign-on instead of hunting across sources; automated 6-month update prompts (pet ownership, contact changes) instead of surveys that "very rarely update"; and a "green light, red light" signal so Jay can see at a glance when a show has everything it needs and is ready to launch to Sales.
2. **Vetting / partner-performance tracking** — Jay uses Marly to track vetting responses and push slow partners; Ryan does the same at the individual-show level and supplies the number that makes it urgent: **shows have 48 hours to return a vetting response** before the brand's budget is typically already spoken for elsewhere ("nine times out of 10 that money is gone"). Ryan currently checks this by hand every other day. He wants an automated alert (approaching/past 48/72 hours) with an option to auto-generate a customizable follow-up email — while explicitly flagging the tension between automating this and preserving the personal relationship he manages with each show. Separately, Ryan (and, per Jay, the rest of the Content Partnerships team) also maintains a manual weekly checklist per show — cross-referencing vetting status and ad-marker completion — and wants a one-click "generate report" per show that pulls stats/billing/approvals into one view instead of hunting across the system; this would also help offset Marly's own slow load times, which today force the team to keep parallel Google Sheets just to avoid losing work to a crash.
3. **Advertiser spot-check & make-good verification** *(new stage this pass — Ryan Middledorf, not mentioned by Jay)* — every Monday, Ryan manually verifies that each advertiser's spots actually aired in their committed week and ran at least 60 seconds (the contracted minimum; ~55 seconds has "a little bit of leeway," ~45 seconds triggers a make-good). He copies the underlying data out of Podscribe into Google Sheets by hand and sends the resulting report to Sales and Ad Ops. He wants the Podscribe API to pull this automatically instead. **This looks like the same class of check Ad Ops's own delivery-reporting stage already performs** (see `journeys/sales-adops/notes.md`, stage 8) — whether these are genuinely redundant or serve different purposes is flagged in [[system-facts]] as an open question, not resolved here.
4. **Billing/invoicing oversight** — monitors partner billing in Marly to spot invoicing gaps and reports back to partners. Jay only; no update from Ryan's interview.
5. **Weekly booking reporting** — every Monday, manually pulls the prior week's bookings out of Marly's order system into a pivot table (Show / Brand / SUM of Amount — a real example, "7/13 Bookings," is now screenshot-confirmed) to notify partners of closed revenue. Same manual export-and-pivot pattern Finance uses for its own monetization sheet.
6. **Custom sponsorship package development** — builds/tracks packages beyond standard spots (segment sponsorships, studio/tour sponsorships, social posts, special interviews, host-specific brand asks) — largely owned by teammate **Annie Hunt** (the Annie Han/Hunt discrepancy from v1 is resolved in favor of Hunt, see [[system-facts]]). Lives entirely outside Marly. Now visually confirmed as (at least) three distinct real artifacts, not one: the "Daylight Custom Opportunities" tracker (one row per show, richer status values than previously assumed); a "custom master sheet" — confirmed real as "Wildmen Presenting Sponsorship Packages," with actual package pricing; and a brand-specific pitch tracker (confirmed real as "Whoop Responses") showing per-show approval status for one specific advertiser's custom pitch. Jay wants the master sheet's rate logic and asset tracking integrated into Marly directly.
7. **One-sheet creation & distribution** — production path is Canva-built PDFs hosted on Google Drive, made by Annie Hunt; a legacy Marly-native one-sheet builder exists but Jay believes it's largely unused ("I don't know how many sellers are actually using the Marley version anymore") — can't export PDF or paste into decks. Unchanged from v1; still the best-documented stage (4 real screenshots).
8. **Ad plan updates** — any change to a show's ad plan currently requires product/"Scott" approval — a bottleneck Jay flagged as not scalable. Unchanged from v1; no update from Ryan's interview.
9. **User access & configuration management** *(new stage this pass — Ryan Middledorf)* — provisioning Marley access for a newly onboarded org is fully manual and per-person: a real recent example, the Daily Beast (6 shows, ~15-20 people needing access), took roughly an hour to provision by hand. Ryan described four access tiers verbally while walking through the real product — "Full Marley" (everything including vetting), "Full Marley... read only" (everything minus vetting), "Marley Light" (no financials, plus vetting), and "Marley Light with no financials" (minus financials and vetting) — see [[system-facts]] for why this may or may not be the same model as the Admin/Manager/Editor/Analyst/Viewer table in `Team Roles.pdf`. For each person: pick their show, set their tier, and Marly generates an invitation email that Ryan then has to manually copy into Gmail and send — one at a time, no bulk option.
10. **Host detail tracking** — granular host info (pets, birthdays, specific sponsor requests) tracked in disparate manual documents, no unified system. Ryan separately described wanting a visual progress tracker (e.g. a completion percentage) for onboarding status per show, which would help with the same "no single place to see status" problem. Still a true gap stage — no screenshot, no system today.
